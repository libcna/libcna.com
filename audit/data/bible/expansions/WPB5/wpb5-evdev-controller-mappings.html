Controller mappings

Which connected device counts as an XNA gamepad, and which of its buttons is A, is decided by SDL3’s gamepad layer, not by CNA. The SDL3 platform enumerates gamepads with SDL_GetGamepads and opens each as an SDL_Gamepad ({{src:modules/platform/src/Sdl3/Sdl3InputServices.cpp|Sdl3InputServices.cpp}}), so the translation from a physical device to the standard layout (face buttons, D-pad, two sticks, two triggers, shoulders, Start, Back and Guide) comes from SDL’s built-in and bundled mapping data. CNA ships and parses no mapping database of its own. A device SDL cannot map is not reported as a gamepad; it still appears in the raw Joysticks view above.

A missing or wrong mapping is therefore corrected at the SDL layer, for example through SDL’s own SDL_GAMECONTROLLERCONFIG environment variable or SDL’s mapping API, without any change to CNA (CNA’s {{src:docs/platform-input-notes.md|platform-input-notes.md}}, “Gamepad backend & mapping”). The one CNA-owned translation point is {{src:modules/platform/src/Sdl3/Sdl3GamepadControls.hpp|Sdl3GamepadControls.hpp}}, which turns SDL’s buttons and axes into CNA’s vocabulary; when a pad first takes a slot, its SDL joystick type is cached and later reported as XNA’s GamePadType.

Evidence. The SDL-to-CNA translation is pinned by Sdl3EventMapperTests (GamepadAxesMapToCnaVocabularyAndBridgeNumerics, EverySupportedGamepadButtonMapsToCnaVocabulary, in {{src:modules/platform/tests/CNA/Platform/Sdl3EventMapperTests.cpp|Sdl3EventMapperTests.cpp}}), and the platform-to-XNA boundary by GamePadMappingTest.EveryButtonMapsToItsXnaFlag ({{src:modules/input/tests/Microsoft/Xna/Framework/Input/GamePadMappingTests.cpp|GamePadMappingTests.cpp}}).
